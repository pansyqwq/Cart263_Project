# Response to Word Nerd

## by Ziyan Pan (Pansy)

https://jakehayduk.github.io/cart263/project-2/

This group decided to continue developing their midterm project, Word Nerd, and the update this time is quite impressive.
The game is now an online multiplayer experience where up to 30 players can join at the same time and compete with each other. There is also a message board that shows who has joined or left the game, and all player messages are stored and visible to everyone. This is really impressive, especially since backend data storage is something we haven’t covered in class yet—it seems like the group members learned it on their own.
With multiplayer gameplay, it can be difficult to distinguish between players, but they handled this well. When logging in, each player can choose from four pixel characters and enter their own name. One feature that really improved my experience was the random color scheme function. It not only assigns each player a unique color tag, but also changes the background color to make those tags stand out more clearly.
My gameplay experience started at the lowest difficulty level with the normal dictionary, and it was very fun with a balanced level of challenge. The coin effect during win streaks was especially lively and engaging. However, there is a small UX issue: the rules of the game are placed inside the settings rather than being introduced at the beginning. It took me a few tries to realize that words must be at least three letters long. While it’s not too hard to figure out, having an introductory screen for new players would improve the experience.
When I tried levels above level 3, the difficulty felt quite high—this might also be related to English not being my first language. Sometimes the word combinations correspond to abbreviations or specialized terms, which makes the game significantly harder.
I also noticed that there are different themed dictionaries, such as birds and hockey players. As a bird enthusiast, I really loved this feature. The variety of dictionaries across levels adds a lot of creativity and greatly enhances the game’s fun.
The group clearly put a lot of effort into the details. I really liked the rotating gear animation when clicking the settings button, as well as the dropdown settings panel. The design is clean and creates a strong contrast against the dark background.
One major bug that significantly affected my experience was when I tried logging in multiple times on the same device to create multiple accounts. The system seemed to break and got stuck on “waiting for the previous player to end the game.” The developers may not have anticipated this scenario.
Overall, this is a very successful and enjoyable mini-game, and I believe it has strong potential to be popular.
